Ray Tracing Fundamentals

Ray tracing is a rendering technique that simulates the path of light rays as they bounce around a scene. This interactive visualization shows the core concept: tracing rays from the camera through pixels, finding intersections with objects, and computing shadows and reflections.

How Ray Tracing Works

1. Cast primary rays: For each pixel on the screen, a ray is shot from the camera through that pixel into the scene. This ray is the primary ray.

2. Find intersections: The ray travels through space until it hits an object. The intersection point is calculated using geometric math (ray-sphere or ray-plane equations).

3. Compute surface normal: At the hit point, we calculate the surface normal (perpendicular direction). This tells us the orientation of the surface.

4. Cast shadow ray: From the hit point, a ray is cast toward the light source (shadow ray). If this ray reaches the light unobstructed, the point is lit. If blocked by another object, it's in shadow.

5. Cast reflection ray: Using the surface normal and incoming ray direction, we compute a reflection ray using the law of reflection: angle in = angle out. This ray continues tracing into the scene.

6. Recursive bounces: The reflection ray can hit another object, where we repeat steps 3-5. This creates realistic reflections and indirect lighting.

Interactive Controls

Trace Rays: Shoots 5-30 rays from the camera (configurable). Watch as primary rays hit objects, shadow rays check for occlusion, and reflection rays bounce off surfaces.

Step-by-Step: Traces a single ray with detailed annotation of each stage: ray origin, intersection, normal computation, shadow test, and reflection.

Toggle displays: Show or hide different ray types to focus on specific concepts.
