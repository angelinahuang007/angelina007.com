How Anti-Aliasing Works

Aliasing occurs because the pixel grid cannot perfectly represent smooth lines and curves. Anti-aliasing solutions:

No AA: Each pixel is fully on or off. Diagonal lines create jagged "stair-step" patterns.

SSAA (Supersampling): Render at higher resolution (4x), then downsample. Provides excellent quality but is expensive.

MSAA (Multisample): Test edges at multiple points within each pixel, blend results. Efficient edge smoothing.

FXAA: Post-process edge detection. Fast but can blur fine details. Detects edges and applies directional blur.

No AA — Raw Rasterization

Each pixel samples only the line's center point. Produces harsh aliasing with visible jaggies on diagonals.

SSAA (4x Supersampling) — Highest Quality

Renders geometry at 4x resolution internally, then downsamples. Extremely smooth but 4-16x more expensive than basic rendering.

MSAA (4x Multisample) — Practical Balance

Samples edges at 4 points per pixel. Much faster than SSAA, good for real-time graphics. Primarily helps edges, not fill areas.

FXAA (Fast Approximate) — Real-time

Post-process filter that detects high-contrast edges and blurs them. Very fast, works on any rendering. May reduce detail sharpness.
